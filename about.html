Hi, I’m Tony.
I build the parts other vendors hand back.

Twenty years of consultative selling and customer success work for IBM, AT&T, and LexisNexis. Now I run TMNMV, a one person consulting and engineering practice that ships websites, marketing, and AI systems for small business operators who need it done, not a proposal about it.

The shape of the work

I sit between the technical and the commercial. That is the whole skill.

Most of my career has been the same job wearing different uniforms. At IBM I ran a sixty account book worth ten million a year, then closed a million dollar single deal and watched it land. At AT&T I ran a Texas territory and grew it sixty nine percent in ninety days. At LexisNexis I was the person a customer called when the data API they bought would not behave. Each of those roles is the same shape: someone hands me a complex technical thing and asks me to make it work for an actual business.

TMNMV is that shape turned on the small business operator. The drilling supplier who needs a website that ranks. The recruiter whose phones ring too much to answer every call. The retail owner whose social media went quiet the day after the launch. I show up, figure out what is actually broken, and ship the thing that fixes it. Sometimes that is a website. Sometimes it is a custom AI assistant wired into a CRM. Sometimes it is a one page sheet the customer takes home.

What I actually do now

The work, listed honestly.

I sell and ship four kinds of work. Each one ends in something the operator can run without me.

Websites and marketing. Clean, fast, mobile friendly sites that rank. One page capability sheets, branded social graphics, the printed piece the customer takes home. Built on a stack the operator can update without calling me.

AI integration. Production AI for real business operations. LLM orchestration, RAG pipelines, AI agents, custom automation. The recent AI assistant for SPM Group is the cleanest example: a custom chat layer wired into the front desk so the recruiters stay on the phones.

IT implementation. Infrastructure, system integration, cloud migration, enterprise tooling. Most of this work is unglamorous: getting the backups running, getting the single sign on working, getting the operator off the personal Gmail and onto something they can hand to their accountant.

AI engineering. Full stack AI product development, prototype through deployment. This is the work I do for my own products and the work I do for operators who need more than a wrapper on someone else’s API.

What I am honest about

Two things worth saying up front.

One. I sold AI and data products at LexisNexis before I built them. I am now closing that gap. The skill I had in that role was API onboarding literacy, not API fluency. I knew how to guide a customer through using an API. I did not yet know how to read or write one. I do now. The work on this site and the AI integrations I ship for customers are how I close that gap, one project at a time.

Two. I write better than I speak. That is a feature, not a bug, for the work I do. The deliverables I ship are written. The AI systems I build are written. The discovery calls and demos happen on the phone and the video call, and they are fine, but the work is the artifact.
